## Overview

This project explores transfer learning for classifying chest X-ray images into two categories: `NORMAL` and `PNEUMONIA`. It uses pre-trained convolutional neural networks as feature extractors and compares VGG16 with InceptionV3.

The dataset is the [Chest X-Ray Images (Pneumonia) dataset on Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/paultimothymooney/chest-xray-pneumonia). The training split is imbalanced, with more pneumonia examples than normal examples.

> This is an educational machine-learning project, not a medical device. Its predictions are not suitable for diagnosis or treatment decisions.

## Workflow

1. Load and inspect the chest X-ray images and their class labels.
2. Resize images to `150 × 150` pixels with three channels and normalize pixel values.
3. Explore class distribution, sample images, and pixel-intensity histograms.
4. Establish a transfer-learning baseline using VGG16.
5. Apply `RandomUnderSampler` to the training data to reduce class imbalance.
6. Train and evaluate VGG16 and InceptionV3 on the undersampled data.
7. Review model performance with classification reports and confusion matrices.

## Models

- **VGG16** — ImageNet-pretrained convolutional base with custom classification layers for the two X-ray classes.
- **InceptionV3** — An alternative ImageNet-pretrained architecture used for comparison.

## Techniques and tools

- **Deep learning:** TensorFlow, Keras, transfer learning, VGG16, and InceptionV3.
- **Image processing:** OpenCV and scikit-image for image loading, resizing, and preprocessing.
- **Data preparation and evaluation:** NumPy, Pandas, scikit-learn, and imbalanced-learn, including `RandomUnderSampler`, classification reports, and confusion matrices.
- **Visualization:** Matplotlib and Seaborn for class distributions, sample images, histograms, and learning curves.

## Scope and limitations

Undersampling reduces the number of examples in the majority class during training; it does not make a model clinically reliable or eliminate other sources of bias. Performance depends on the dataset, split, preprocessing, and evaluation protocol. No clinical validation or performance metrics are provided here.
